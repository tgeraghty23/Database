# From an ambiguous research objective to a useful answer

4 November 2026 • Operating method v0.4 • Working guidance; effectiveness not yet established • v0.3 (30 October) with five practices integrated after the services-map retrospective in `10_retrospective_services-map_2026-11-04/`; no new mandatory artefact

## The proposition

Good research explains something consequential clearly enough that a reader can use it. It answers the intended question, shows why its conclusions follow, distinguishes evidence from inference, and identifies the conditions that would change the answer.

Its purpose is to find the best available understanding of the matter, not to produce a persuasive confirmation of the user's or researcher's starting view. That includes discovering facts, assumptions, connections and alternative framings neither anticipated. A clear answer is valuable because its reasoning can be understood and challenged, not because clarity makes it true.

An AI research process should take responsibility for reaching that result. The user should not have to supply every important example, choose every investigation or reconcile competing drafts. The user supplies the purpose and preferences that cannot be inferred; a research owner designs and runs the inquiry within that mandate.

The proposed method has a sequential core: understand the assignment, orient, develop explanations, investigate, integrate and communicate. Investigations can branch or run in parallel. Integration determines what happens next. This is a starting design, not a proven best practice or a requirement to produce a separate document for every step.

## What good looks like

| Dimension | A good answer | Failure to detect |
|---|---|---|
| Relevance | Answers the governing question for its intended use | A tractable proxy silently replaces the question |
| Explanation | Connects causes, conditions and consequences | Examples or correlations substitute for reasoning |
| Discovery | Finds consequential facts, distinctions or alternatives | Repeats familiar discourse or depends on user-supplied counterexamples |
| Grounding | Sources support the claims actually made | Promotional claims become observed outcomes; uncertainty is hidden |
| Truth-seeking | Actively tests assumptions and seeks inconvenient evidence | A coherent answer survives only because contrary evidence or alternative framings were not sought |
| Judgment | Weighs explanations and says what follows | Everything remains an unranked possibility |
| Clarity | The reader can restate the argument | Jargon, caveats and examples obscure the causal steps |
| Usefulness | Changes understanding, priorities or a decision | Findings have no explained consequence |
| Efficiency | Achieves quality with proportionate effort | Unnecessary research, user mediation or review loops |

Originality is not required for its own sake. Confirming an important proposition can be valuable. A surprising finding earns its place through consequence and support, not novelty alone.

## Who decides what

One research owner is accountable for the question, research design, assignments, synthesis and final answer. Researchers and reviewers can propose changes, but do not independently redefine the objective. The owner records material changes with reasons and tells the user when they change the intended deliverable.

The owner decides routine methods, sources, reversible assumptions, sequencing and stopping. The user decides consequential preferences, desired use and scope changes that cannot safely be inferred. Available records and connected sources should be consulted before asking for information they may already contain.

Private access, practitioner interviews and external messages require the relevant access and authorisation. Missing access is a limitation to state, not a reason to invent evidence or stop all independent work.

## 1. Establish the assignment

Write a short brief in ordinary language: the question, intended reader and use, what the answer must explain, provisional scope and material unknowns. Distinguish an exploratory question from an investment decision, causal inquiry, forecast or implementation recommendation. One project can contain several, but they need different evidence.

Do not force the user to choose a business or investment merely to begin an exploratory inquiry. Do ask when different plausible objectives would produce materially different work. Explain the choice, recommend a default and identify its consequence.

**Move on when:** the owner can explain what would count as a useful answer and what would constitute answering the wrong question.

## 2. Discover enough context to design the inquiry

Read existing material, then conduct a bounded orientation. Look for the concepts and distinctions needed to understand the problem, current developments, strong existing explanations, historical constraints and contrasting cases. Seek sources beyond those supplied by the user.

Select the mix according to the question. Frontier deployments are especially relevant to emerging possibilities; representative data matters for prevalence; historical cases can illuminate adoption or incentives. None should automatically dominate every inquiry.

Record what this exploration changes about the framing. Absence of a source is not absence of a phenomenon. A frontier case can reveal a possible mechanism without establishing that it generalises.

Reserve some exploration for improving the questions themselves. Ask what the user, owner and sources take for granted; which assumptions would change the conclusion if false; and whether another discipline, timescale or unit of analysis reveals something missing. Follow promising connections, then explain their relevance to the governing objective. Do not require every discovery to fit an existing hypothesis, or treat novelty as evidence of importance.

**Move on when:** plausible explanations, consequential uncertainties and informative evidence routes can be identified. Do not wait to understand the whole field. Reopen orientation if later evidence exposes a missing part of the problem.

## 3. Write the provisional argument

Draft the answer before commissioning substantial research. State the mechanism, conditions and consequences, alongside serious alternatives. For a forecast, distinguish present evidence from capability assumptions and future scenarios. For a causal question, identify rival causes. For a feasibility question, identify the complete arrangement needed to deliver the result.

Keep a route for findings that none of the initial explanations predicts. An early thesis focuses research but can also anchor it. At integration, ask explicitly what lies outside it.

For the strongest materially different answer, state what would make it right. Keep the user's purpose, the explanation and a particular proposed investment or product separate: rejecting one construction does not reject every way of meeting the purpose.

**Move on when:** the owner can name observations that would change the argument. A provisional answer need not be confident, polished or numerical.

## 4. Decide what deserves research

Prioritise by consequence, uncertainty and tractability: how much could this answer change the conclusion; what is genuinely unknown; and can available work discriminate between explanations? Consider cost, dependencies and the risk of neglecting a whole category. Use qualitative rankings unless numerical estimates have a defensible basis.

Each assignment should specify:

- The question and its connection to the governing objective.
- Competing explanations and what would distinguish them.
- Method and evidence needed, including relevant negative cases.
- Inputs, scope, effort boundary and stopping condition.
- Required return: answer, supporting evidence, limitations, implication and any challenge to the assignment itself.

Do not research a topic merely because it has abundant sources. Stop an investigation when further feasible work is unlikely to change the answer enough to justify its cost. Before any further round, including one proposed by a reviewer or the user, state which consequential uncertainty it would resolve and how either result would change the explanation, the opportunity set or the practical recommendation, and weigh that against the effort. Improving understanding counts; an immediate decision is not required. If no such change can be named, conclude provisionally and record the open question with its route to learning. If decisive direct evidence is inaccessible, consider triangulation, analogous evidence, sensitivity analysis or an explicit best judgment. Explain what these can and cannot establish; missing direct evidence does not automatically require withholding direction.

## 5. Choose how the work is structured

| Structure | Use when | Integration requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Sequential | Later work depends on a framing choice or finding | Inspect the output before designing the next step |
| Parallel branches | Questions are sufficiently independent | Shared definitions, comparable returns and an owner who reconciles them |
| Independent attempts | Competing explanations or designs matter | Compare mechanisms and evidence, not votes or writing styles |
| Focused specialist | A bounded technical or domain question needs depth | State how its answer changes the whole inquiry |
| Practitioner input | Crucial process facts or tacit knowledge are unavailable in records | Distinguish reported experience from general evidence |
| Multiple models | A distinct perspective or capability has a specific job | Track material contributions and coordination cost |

A fresh-context pass within the session's existing tools (a source check of the decisive claims, a reasoning check, an independent discovery pass or a blind restatement of the opening) is a control the owner may use without asking when its expected contribution justifies the overhead; it is not required for every delivery, and whether it reduces later correction is not yet established. Practitioner interviews, external reviewers, outreach, account connections and paid data need authorisation. A swarm is not an objective. Additional agents increase useful capacity only if tasks can be separated and their outputs evaluated and combined. They can also multiply a shared mistake.

Assign one writer per artifact. Reserve filenames before writing. Other contributors submit proposals or reviews; they do not overwrite the owned file. Preserve source material and maintain one short authoritative state note. Do not duplicate the entire history into every working context.

### Nested inquiries

A large project can contain smaller projects, each with its own orientation, argument, investigation and conclusion. Create a subproject when a consequential question needs a distinct method or sustained inquiry; do not turn every task into another project with its own paperwork.

Keep a simple dependency map: which conclusions require which inputs, which definitions must be shared, and where findings feed back into other branches. A subproject returns its answer, evidence, unresolved assumptions, implications for other branches and conditions requiring reopening. Completion means usable at a stated level of confidence, not permanently settled.

The final synthesis is not a bundle of summaries. The owner must reconcile interactions: cheaper implementation could alter an organisational comparison; expanded demand could overturn a labour forecast. Avoid double-counting benefits, circular support between branches and multiplying confidence across dependent claims. If one result changes a material premise elsewhere, reopen the affected reasoning rather than mechanically preserving completed work.

## 6. Integrate, then choose the next action

After a meaningful research round, update the argument: what changed, why, what survived, what was contradicted and what is still missing. Check both the local findings and whether the overall inquiry still serves the objective.

Choose explicitly: conclude; investigate a consequential gap; revise the explanation; or reopen the framing. Do not default to another research round because uncertainty remains. Nor should an elegant argument terminate work when a readily testable premise is decisive.

Check dependencies. Multiple sources may repeat one company claim; several agents may share the same assumptions. Agreement is not automatically independent support.

Before ranking alternatives, compare their decisive advantages and weaknesses on compatible definitions. Give the favoured replacement the scrutiny applied to the rejected option. Equal scrutiny does not require equal research budgets: expose consequential differences in evidence and distinguish a priority for investigation from a preferred investment or action.

When a premise changes, locate the claims that depend on it in the summary, calculations, recommendation, proposed tests and active handover. Do this by searching every active output (the document, its companions, record fields, cards and the handover) for the claim's terms and listing the passages found; revise each or record why it still holds, then check those passages against each other. The list is the evidence that the consequences were checked. Recording that a correction was accepted is not.

## 7. Write the explanation and review it in both directions

The executive summary should make the answer understandable without opening the appendix: claim, reasons, conditions, implications and consequential uncertainty. Begin sections with their argument. Use an example when it explains or tests a particular step, not to signal that extensive research occurred.

Replace vague qualification with conditional reasoning. Instead of "it depends on implementation", explain which implementation costs or actions determine the outcome and why. Keep exact figures when they change the judgment; move supporting inventories to linked evidence notes.

Review for both overstatement and omission. Check unsupported claims, alternative causes and omitted costs; also check important possibilities ignored and conclusions narrower than the evidence warrants. A valid criticism need not invent a replacement claim, but the owner must decide what the criticism does to the best available answer.

Clear prose is a reasoning test. Terms need stable meanings, comparisons need explicit baselines, and each paragraph should advance the explanation. Neither confident tone nor extensive caveats substitute for judgment. Before delivery, check that a reader can take the answer, its reasons, its implications and its main uncertainty from the opening without reconstructing missing links: a term whose object is unstated, a priority whose reason is elsewhere, an implication left to inference. Rewrite where that fails. Keep diagnostic counts, correction history and review records out of the main answer; they belong in the run note or an appendix.

### Specialised review

Use review roles according to the risks of the assignment. They may be separate agents when authorised and useful, or distinct review passes. They are not four mandatory agents for every project.

| Role | Assignment and inputs | When it matters most |
|---|---|---|
| Evidence reviewer | Test exact claims against original sources; inspect independence, denominators, selection and omitted qualifications | Conclusions depend on contested facts, numerical claims or reused source summaries |
| Reasoning reviewer | Examine causal steps, baselines, hidden premises, alternative causes and sensitivity | The answer makes explanatory, economic or forecasting claims |
| Discovery challenger | Start from the original objective; propose missing mechanisms, inconvenient cases and alternative frames before comparing with the draft | The inquiry risks confirming its framing or missing a consequential possibility |
| Writing reviewer | Test whether the reader can follow the argument; clarify and compress without strengthening or weakening claims | A substantive draft is ready; a brief early pass can also expose confused reasoning |

Ask each reviewer for the precise defect or omitted possibility, why it matters, its evidence or reasoning, a proposed repair where available, and whether new investigation is needed. Prioritise consequences over numbers of comments. Before editing, record in the run note what each item is, a demonstrated defect with its evidence, an alternative interpretation or preferred replacement, or a proposal for further research, and the decision on it with the reason. A reviewer's proposed next step is a proposal and goes through the test in section 4 before any work starts. Accepting every demonstrated defect can be right; what matters is that the distinction was made and recorded, not how many items were declined. The owner accepts, rejects or investigates material findings with reasons. Resolve substantive disagreements before polishing prose; recheck claims whose meaning changes during editing.

Review is not only a final gate. A discovery challenge can change case selection before research; a source check can stop an invalid inference while it is being built. Preserve appropriate independence: original sources for evidence checks, and the original objective without the preferred answer initially for an alternative framing. A second model or majority vote does not establish truth. Review agents can make errors too.

### Check the claims that determine the answer

Before delivery, identify the few claims whose failure would change the answer or action. For each, check the supporting source or calculation, its scope and the inference drawn, and write beside it the sentence as it stands in the document and the sentence the source supports; where they differ, rewrite the document's sentence rather than qualifying it. A check whose result stays in the check table and not in the text has not been applied. Prioritise these over polishing peripheral facts. This is the owner's responsibility; a separate agent is optional. A follow-up that introduces a new model, recommendation or decisive premise needs these checks even if the parent report was reviewed.

For a quantitative conclusion, reproduce the decisive calculation and test the prose against its actual outputs. Separate sourced inputs, assumed inputs and derived results. Ask whether the result follows from evidence or from the model's construction. In changing markets, check stocks versus flows, timing, retention and the population actually modelled. Normalise material comparison differences such as period, entity, scope and earnings definition, or explain their effect. A coarse sensitivity grid does not establish an exact threshold; a counterexample within the model defeats an "only if" claim. More detailed checks are in `MODEL_CHECKS.md`, for use when relevant.

Test broad exclusion claims as carefully as positive ones. "Not found" is not "does not exist"; acquired assets do not by themselves exhaust possible entry routes; a conflict or procurement condition is not automatically a legal prohibition. State the specific boundary the evidence supports. Review findings themselves need checking before adoption.

## Reaching a judgment under uncertainty

Use the strongest conclusion warranted by the task and evidence. Distinguish the following in plain prose; labels are aids to reasoning, not mandatory tags on every sentence.

| Conclusion | What it means |
|---|---|
| Established finding | Appropriate evidence supports the particular claim within a stated scope |
| Best current judgment | Incomplete evidence and reasoning favour one explanation or direction over alternatives |
| Conditional conclusion | The implication follows if specified assumptions hold; their plausibility must be assessed separately |
| Scenario | A coherent possibility explored without asserting it is the most likely |
| Open question | There is no defensible direction yet, and the reason matters to the answer |

When direction is required, state: my current view is X; A and B outweigh C; the weakest premise is D; E would change the view; until then the implication is F. This is not permission to manufacture facts or confidence. Sometimes no direction is warranted: say why, and consider whether a robust decision is possible across the remaining alternatives. Numerical probabilities are useful only when their basis and sensitivity can be explained.

A decision to pass can be justified by uncertainty, price, access or opportunity cost without proving the thesis false. Say which reason drives the decision. An assumption-driven scenario can identify a threshold worth testing; it cannot establish that the real world crosses it. Preserve a best current judgment after correction rather than replacing every conclusion with an open question.

For a consequential unknown, identify its kind and the route to learning. An undisclosed fact may be obtainable from records; a causal effect may need a comparison or experiment; a future outcome may require observation over time; a preference or conflict may require elicitation or agreement rather than more factual research. Some uncertainty may remain irreducible. State what could be known, by what means and at what approximate effort where assessable. Do not call something unknowable merely because this project has not found it.

## User attention and stopping

Retrieve available facts; decide reversible research choices; ask for material preferences or inaccessible private information only when they affect the answer. Bundle questions and continue independent work. State assumptions where a wrong assumption would matter. Do not repeatedly request approval for an already authorised task.

Conclude when the answer is useful for its stated purpose, major contradictions have been addressed, consequential uncertainties are visible, and further feasible research is unlikely to change the answer enough to justify its cost. Being able to state a mechanism is not sufficient: investigate its consequential premises. Deliver the strongest warranted conclusion, including explicit judgment where the objective calls for direction. State what would change it and whether that information is obtainable. If a budget or access limit ends the work earlier, distinguish that practical stop from evidential completion.

## How to test this method

Use a fresh bounded assignment. Before starting, record the intended deliverable, effort budget and evaluation criteria. Keep a minimal event record of framing changes, assignments, substantive corrections and user interventions. Do not require the user to maintain it.

Assess each quality dimension as weak, adequate or strong, with reasons and examples. Assess clarity as reader reconstruction: whether the opening can be restated with its reasons, implications and main uncertainty, and how much had to be inferred. Do not collapse them into one score that lets polished prose compensate for factual failure. Ask the reader for one overall assessment and the most consequential defect, rather than many administrative approvals.

Record user corrections by type: new preference, inaccessible fact, missed available context, wrong inference, scope drift or unclear writing. Count avoidable supervision separately from valuable new input. Record time and tool use where available; otherwise leave them unknown.

Include consequential corrections and external review after first delivery in the assessment of supervision. Keep the original delivery assessment dated rather than treating later repairs as first-pass success. A new commission or genuinely new private context is not automatically avoidable supervision. These observations support process lessons, not a ranking of models working in different roles.

Also record whether discovery or review changed the framing, explanation or conclusion; which consequential errors it caught or introduced; and the extra coordination it required. Review is valuable through those effects, not because agents agreed or generated many comments. Assess whether the final judgment communicates direction at the confidence warranted, rather than rewarding certainty or caution by itself.

One successful assignment shows feasibility, not repeatability. Across later assignments, compare similar difficulty and intended use. Where feasible, compare outputs under the same resources with a simpler baseline and have a reader assess them without knowing the method. This project is a retrospective source of hypotheses, not a controlled baseline.

Revise or simplify the method if it produces more paperwork without better explanations, repeatedly misses available decisive context, or depends on the user to reconstruct its reasoning. Encode only the useful, tested parts into a skill. Do not add a permanent rule after every isolated incident.
