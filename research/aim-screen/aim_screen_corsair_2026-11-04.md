# AIM screen for Corsair Capital: investable assets on London's junior market

4 November 2026. Research note produced under METHOD.md v0.4. Data files: `data/aim_screen_shortlist_2026-11-04.csv`,
`data/aim_screen_removed_2026-11-04.csv`, `data/aim_universe_stockanalysis_2026-11-04.csv`; evidence in `agent-returns/`.

## Summary

AIM offers Corsair a short list, not a pipeline. Of roughly 595 AIM companies, about 55 sit in Corsair's three verticals
(payments; software; business services including wealth and asset management, insurance services, professional services,
healthcare services, trust and corporate services). After removing names that have left AIM, are under offer, are
balance-sheet lenders or investment companies, or fall below the USD 150m enterprise-value floor, six names remain that
are on-vertical, inside the USD 150m to 750m band and have no blocking shareholder: Boku, Tatton Asset Management,
FRP Advisory, Fintel, BTG Consulting (the renamed Begbies Traynor) and Netcall. A further ten are on-vertical and in
band but carry one specific obstacle: a founder or concert-party block, a regulatory ownership rule, leverage, or a
foreign primary listing. Around a dozen more fit Corsair's themes only as add-ons to an existing platform.

The reason the list is short is that the market has already been worked. Since January 2024, at least 17 AIM companies
in financial services, fintech, wealth, insurance or professional services have been bid for, mostly by financial
sponsors (Pollen Street, Bridgepoint, STG, TA, General Atlantic, TowerBrook and J.C. Flowers, Harwood, DBAY, Long Path)
and US strategics (FirstCash twice, Corpay). Clean recommended cash bids cleared at 26 to 45 percent premiums; insider-led
take-privates at 11 to 18 percent. Two of the largest financial names (GB Group, Mortgage Advice Bureau) moved to the Main
Market in the last year rather than sell. What is left on AIM is therefore either priced for growth (Tatton, Netcall),
priced for a problem (Fintel's leverage, Impax's outflows, Beeks' cash burn), or a people business where licences and
partners, not the plc, are the asset (FRP, BTG, the law firms).

My current view is that Boku and Fintel deserve first-look work now, Tatton and FRP are worth a relationship but not a
bid at today's prices, and the professional-services names are cheap for a reason Corsair should test rather than assume.
The weakest premise is the mandate itself: Corsair's EV range and deal types come from a third-party summary of its
website, and no Corsair UK public-to-private could be found. If Corsair will not do UK schemes, the useful residue is the
add-on list and a minority or PIPE conversation with the founder-controlled names.

## 1. The filter

Corsair's buyouts platform describes itself as a financial-services specialist investing in three verticals with
sub-sectors: payments (integrated, international, B2B); software (vertical and enterprise, risk and compliance, HR and
finance tools); business services (wealth and asset management, insurance services, professional services, healthcare
services, trust and corporate services). It targets enterprise values of USD 150m to 750m, larger with co-investors,
and does platform, add-on and minority deals with control or real influence. Fund VI closed around April 2024 at roughly
USD 470m to 500m on public filings, well below Fund V's USD 1.0bn, and a USD 600m continuation vehicle (May 2025) holds
Zedra, Redpin and IDnow. Corsair European Insurance Services is a Fund VI broker aggregator whose first deal was in
Poland. The London office is led by the head of European buyouts.

Three consequences for the screen. First, the sterling band is roughly GBP 115m to 575m of enterprise value at 1.30, so
most AIM financials are too small. Second, a smaller fund plus co-invest habit means the top of the band is reachable
only with partners. Third, add-ons for IDnow (identity), Redpin (property payments) and CEIS (insurance distribution) are
a legitimate second route, and the existing platforms tell us which small names matter.

The IHT change removes part of the retail bid that supported AIM valuations, the rule changes make the market friendlier
to acquisitive issuers, and sponsors and overseas buyers are described in every 2026 commentary as the marginal buyer.
None of this creates assets; it lowers the price of the ones that exist and shortens the time they stay quoted.

## 4. What the precedents say about price and competition

| Pattern | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Clean recommended cash bids | 26 to 45 percent premiums: Gresham 27, FD Technologies 27, Marlowe 26.5, Ramsdens 33, Mattioli Woods 34, LTG 34, Equals 37, H&T 44 |
| Insider or controller-led | 11 to 18 percent: Eckoh 11, Frenkel Topping 13, Anexo 18; Kingswood swept at a nominal price |
| Strategic buyers paid most | Aquis/SIX 120 percent, Alpha/Corpay 55 percent, FirstCash 33 to 44 percent |
| Disclosed multiples | Gresham 12.9x adjusted EBITDA; Eckoh 15.9x; Idox c.21x cash EBITDA; Ramsdens c.6 to 7x PBT |
| Failed or contested | Argentex lapsed into administration; Team Internet approaches withdrawn; H&T rejected four proposals first; Idox had to switch from scheme to offer |

Applied to Tier 1 at a 35 percent premium: Boku would cost about GBP 400m EV, or 12 to 13x FY26 EBITDA, in line with the
software precedents; Fintel about GBP 290m EV, or 9 to 10x pro-forma EBITDA; FRP about GBP 375m, or 8x; BTG about GBP
245m, or 7.5x; Tatton about GBP 520m, or 18x adjusted operating profit, which is why Tatton is a relationship rather than
a bid. Competition is specific: Pollen Street and Harwood are the sponsors that have shown up repeatedly in UK wealth
and services names, and Harwood already sits on the registers of Optima, Restore and Polar.

## 5. Implications

- First-look work: Boku (payments, dispersed register, self-identified as undervalued) and Fintel (on-strategy, founder
  catalyst, balance sheet that a sponsor can fix).
- Relationship, not bid: Tatton and FRP. Both are good businesses whose current price or structure needs a reason to move.
- Test the cheapness: BTG, Knights, Keystone. Low multiples reflect personal licences, SRA ownership rules and
  cash-conversion doubts. If Corsair's professional-services thesis has an answer to those, the discount is real.
- Add-on list: Intercede for IDnow is the clearest; the others are small and should be run through the platforms' teams.
- Minority or PIPE: Keystone, Optima, Personal Group, Fintel where control is blocked but capital or an exit for a
  large holder might be welcome.
- Carve-out angle (alternative framing kept open): Defaqto inside Fintel, Paradigm inside Tatton, the broking arm inside
  Property Franchise Group, and B.P. Marsh's investees are the assets most like Corsair's existing portfolio.

## 6. Uncertainty and what would change the view

- Corsair's mandate figures (EV band, deal types) come from a third-party summary; its own site states verticals and
  deal types but not the band. If the floor is lower, Tier 3 becomes platform material. Obtainable from Corsair directly.
- No Corsair UK public-to-private was found. If UK schemes are out of policy, the answer narrows to add-ons and minorities.
- Bid-approach checks for Fintel, Polar Capital, Mercia and Cavendish were not completed because the session's search
  budget ran out; a current offer period would change the ranking. Obtainable from the Takeover Panel disclosure table.
- Ownership for Personal Group, Beeks and Fonix rests on aggregators rather than company Rule 26 pages.
- Market caps are one day's delayed prices; the ranking does not turn on any single figure.

Established findings: which names are on AIM, their latest results and the precedent premiums. Best current judgment:
the ranking and the first-look picks. Conditional: the price arithmetic, which assumes a 35 percent premium and GBP/USD
of 1.30. Open question: whether Corsair wants UK take-privates at all.
