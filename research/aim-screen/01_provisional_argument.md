# Provisional argument (written before the verification round, 4 November 2026)

## Claim
AIM is a thin but real source of Corsair-type assets. The pool of companies in Corsair's verticals above about
GBP 100m has been picked over by take-privates since 2023, so what remains is either (a) a handful of mid-sized
names with a specific reason they have not yet gone private (founder control, regulatory capital, a recent
disappointment), or (b) sub-scale names that only make sense as add-ons to an existing Corsair platform.

## Mechanism
- Supply side: AIM has shrunk from 1,694 companies (2007) to roughly 600; takeovers were the largest exit cause in
  2025 (28 of 84 departures), with PE and overseas buyers active. The halving of inheritance-tax business relief
  from April 2026 removed part of the retail bid for AIM shares, which weakens valuations and the case for staying listed.
- Demand side: specialist financial-services sponsors (Pollen Street, JC Flowers/TowerBrook, Searchlight, Bridgepoint,
  Harwood, Long Path) have already taken Mattioli Woods, Equals, Gresham House, Alfa FMC, Eckoh, Frenkel Topping and
  others private. Corsair competes with these on the same names.
- Corsair's filter: EV USD 150m to 750m; payments, software (risk and compliance, HR and finance tools, vertical),
  business services (wealth and asset management, insurance services, professional services, trust and corporate
  services, healthcare services). This maps to perhaps 40 to 55 AIM names before size and ownership screens.

## Expected result
- Genuine platform candidates: few, probably under ten. Likely names: Tatton Asset Management, Fintel, Polar Capital,
  Boku, FRP Advisory, Mortgage Advice Bureau (if still quoted), GB Group (if still quoted), Keystone Law, Restore.
- Add-on candidates for existing Corsair platforms: identity verification names for IDnow; payments names for Redpin;
  insurance distribution for Corsair European Insurance Services; trust and corporate services for Zedra (probably none on AIM).
- Minority or PIPE: founder-controlled names where control is not for sale but growth capital might be.

## Serious alternatives
- A1: The pool is better than this. Depressed AIM valuations mean several GBP 100m to 500m names in Corsair's verticals
  are available at single-digit EBITDA multiples and simply have not been approached. What would make it right:
  several candidates with no bid history, dispersed ownership and valuation below precedent multiples.
- A2: The pool is worse than this. Everything of quality has gone or is under offer, and the survivors have structural
  reasons (regulatory capital, founder blocks, litigation exposure) that make PE ownership unattractive. What would
  make it right: most candidates fail on ownership or regulatory flags; precedent buyers were mostly strategics.
- A3: The right unit is not the AIM company but the carve-out. The best assets may be divisions of AIM groups
  (for example a software unit inside a services group) rather than whole companies.

## Observations that would change the argument
- Corsair has a stated policy against UK public-to-private deals (would reduce the list to minority and add-on only).
- Precedent premiums above 50 percent and EV/EBITDA above 12x for financial-services AIM targets (would shift the
  recommendation toward add-ons and away from platforms).
- A candidate under offer or in a formal sale process at the time of writing (removes it).
