# Professional services: where Corsair could extend coverage

Working note, 22 October 2026. Current coverage: wealth management, insurance brokerage, trust & corporate services (ZEDRA). The Swedish business-law work in this repo (MAQS) is treated here as a live candidate rather than existing coverage.

Sources are trade press, sponsor websites and sector reports gathered on 22 October 2026. Deal terms are as reported, not verified against filings.

## 1. The short answer

Corsair's three existing verticals share one economic shape: regulated, recurring, advice-plus-administration businesses sold to wealthy individuals and mid-market corporates, with fragmented supply and owner-led targets. ## 2. What the peer set actually does

**Waterland** (Benelux-rooted, pan-European). Professional services deals 2024-2026: Moore DRV and De Jong & Laan (NL accountancy, 2024), Moore Kingston Smith and then Moore Ireland (2024), Cooper Parry (UK, grown from ~GBP 45m to ~GBP 180m, sold to Lee Equity 2025), COGEP (France, minority), Auren Spain (audit, legal, corporate finance, 2025), NFP Group (employee benefits, 2024), Beyond Law Group (UK legal, 2024), NLO/Ipsilon (patent attorneys, 2025), Chemservice and EBRC (regulatory compliance, 2025-26), Black & White Engineering and ZICHT (engineering consultancy), Minsait business consulting (2026). In October 2026 it folded six firms including Moore Kingston Smith and Moore Ireland into Auren, a ~EUR 1bn integrated group. Pattern: accountancy-led, then bolt adjacent disciplines (legal, IP, benefits) onto the same mid-market client base.

**Ufenau** (Swiss, DACH plus Iberia, Poland, Benelux, UK). Ufenau VIII closed at EUR 2.12bn in 2025. 52 acquisitions in 2025 alone (3 platforms, 49 add-ons). The three 2025 platforms were all professional services: PKF WMS (German tax, legal, M&A advisory, now with nbs partners and FACT added), KWKR (Polish law firm) and Express Solicitors (UK personal-injury law, 800 staff). Earlier it built and exited Fair Damage Control (insurance claims handling). Pattern: small regional firms bought at low entry multiples, integrated into a multidisciplinary group, 26% sales CAGR across the portfolio.

**Nordic sponsors.** Axcel/AGRD (Swedish law, 2025 onward, Maria-Pia Hope as CEO). PAI and Hg/Azets (90+ bolt-ons, bought part of KPMG Sweden's SME audit and tax business in 2025, SEK 1bn Swedish revenue). Vitruvian/Aspia (SEK 3.5bn revenue, Accountor merged in, Danish add-ons 2025). TowerBrook/ECIT. Altor/Ludvig & Co. IK Partners built Vistra (corporate services) and Aspia originally. EQT/WTS (German tax, EUR 250m revenue, 50-55% stake, "AI-native tax champion").

**Financial-services specialists closest to Corsair.** Lovell Minnick (Broadstone, pensions consulting, 2025). Aquiline (Isio, 2024). Cinven (Miller insurance broker 2021, Grant Thornton UK, Alter Domus). Apax (S&W from Evelyn Partners, 2024). Genstar (ACA Group compliance). Warburg/Permira (sold Evelyn Partners wealth to NatWest for GBP 2.7bn, 2026). These firms are already treating pensions consulting, accountancy and compliance as financial services, which is the precedent for Corsair doing the same.

## 3. A Corsair-specific angle

Ufenau and Waterland are generalist service-company consolidators. Corsair should not compete as a generalist. The differentiated story is a **private-client and mid-market financial-advice ecosystem**: wealth management, insurance broking, trust & corporate services, and then tax advisory, pensions/benefits consulting and private-client law serving the same families, entrepreneurs and mid-market companies. Each addition should be defensible on one of three tests:

1. **Same client, new fee line.** Tax, pensions and private-client law all sell to the clients wealth managers and trust companies already hold.
2. **Same regulator-driven moat.** Compliance consulting, fund admin add-ons, claims handling.
3. **Same buy-and-build mechanics as ZEDRA.** Fragmented, owner-led, sub-EUR 50m revenue targets at single-digit entry multiples, integrated into a platform that exits at a scale premium.

Geography: UK accountancy and pensions are the most picked-over markets in Europe. The Nordics, DACH, Benelux and Ireland still have independent firms at reasonable prices, and Corsair's MAQS work already gives it Nordic sourcing infrastructure.

2. Map Swedish and Nordic tax advisory (Skeppsbron Skatt, Svalner, Unum Tax and the Big Four SME carve-outs) against Azets and Aspia to find what is still independent.
3. Decide whether AGRD is a competitor or an eventual counterparty for the MAQS thesis.
4. Test the compliance-consulting idea with ZEDRA management: which services their clients already buy from third parties.
