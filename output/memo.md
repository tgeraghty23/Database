# Investment memo: private higher education operators in Europe

22 October 2026. Prepared for a control-buyout mandate. Companion files: `operators.xlsx`, `precedents.xlsx`, `country_matrix.xlsx`, `market_map.md`, `sources.md`.

## Assumptions

The brief left some parameters open. This memo assumes:

- Enterprise value EUR 50m to 300m, control deals, as confirmed.
- Equity cheque EUR 30m to 150m, consistent with 3x to 5x leverage on EUR 10m to 40m EBITDA.
- Five-year hold, 2.5x money multiple and 20% IRR hurdle.
- All European geographies weighted equally, as confirmed. The country attractiveness ratings in `country_matrix.xlsx` then drive the ordering.
- No hard exclusions beyond the scope definition. Accredited vocational and professional groups are included because they are where most sponsor activity below EUR 300m has happened. K-12, language schools and pure software remain out.
- Figures are as reported in the cited source. Where only an estimate exists it is labelled as such. Roughly a quarter of the 203 operators have a disclosed revenue figure.

## 2. Demand drivers

- Public capacity constraints. Spain, Italy and Poland cap or ration public places in medicine, health sciences and business; private operators fill the gap at full price. Spanish privates now take 53% of master's enrolment.
- Online and hybrid adoption. Italy's telematic segment, Germany's distance-learning providers (IU, AKAD, HFH), UNIR and Unicaf show double-digit growth where the licence permits scale. The regulatory response (Italy's DM 1835, Germany's §16b ruling, Spain's online-university approval by parliament) is now the main constraint on this driver.
- International students. The UK, Cyprus, Poland, Germany and the Netherlands have relied on non-EU recruitment. 2024 to 2026 policy in all five has tightened. Operators with majority-domestic intake (Lumi, Cesur, IRO, Metropolitan College, UCJC) carry less of this risk.
- Apprenticeship and public funding in France. The 2018 reform built groups like Eduservices (32k of 40k students on apprenticeships) and Groupe Alternance; the cuts to employer aid from March 2026 and to distance-CFA funding from July 2025 are reversing the tailwind. Entries fell 16.4% to May 2026.
- Professional and health licences. Medicine (IRO, UniCamillus, Humanitas, UAX, UCAM), law (ULaw, BPP), hospitality (Sommet, SEG, EHL) and music or film (BIMM) command pricing power because the degree is a licence to practise or a scarce brand.

## 3. Consolidation history, 2015 to 2026

`precedents.xlsx` holds 168 rows. The pattern has three phases.

Phase one, 2015 to 2018: Laureate's European exit. Laureate sold Glion and Les Roches to Eurazeo (CHF 380m, c. 13.6x, 2016), Universidad Europea to Permira (EUR 770m, 2018), its Italian and Cyprus schools to Galileo (EUR 225m, 2017) and its German universities to GUS (2018). Galileo under Providence bought Studialis (EUR 250m, 2015). Oakley bought Career Partner Group, now IU, for EUR 85m of equity for 79% on EUR 10m EBITDA (2017).

Phase two, 2019 to 2022: large-cap platforms. CVC bought UAX (c. EUR 1.1bn, 2019) and 50% of Multiversity (EUR 225m, 10x to 15x, 2019; the rest in 2021 at c. USD 1.6bn). Cinven bought INSEEC, now Omnes (c. EUR 800m, 13x to 14x, 2019). Téthys, CPPIB, Montagu and Bpifrance bought Galileo from Providence (EUR 2.3bn to 2.5bn, c. 18x, 2020). Ardian bought AD Education (EUR 600m, c. 19x to 20x, 2020) and Study Group (c. GBP 500m, 2019). TDR bought BPP (c. GBP 700m, 2021). KKR bought MasterD and MEDAC (EUR 150m and EUR 200m). IK bought Skill & You (2021), ICG bought BIMM (2020), Galileo bought AKAD (>EUR 45m, 2022).

Phase three, 2023 to 2026: secondaries, continuation funds and stress. CVC moved Multiversity into a continuation fund at EUR 3.97bn (2024). Oakley moved IU into a continuation vehicle (2023) and wrote it down in 2025-26. EQT Infrastructure bought 64% of Universidad Europea at c. EUR 2.0bn to 2.2bn, c. 20x (2024). TDR's BPP auction at GBP 2.5bn failed (2024). Cinven and Mubadala bought UAX from CVC at c. EUR 2.0bn, c. 17x (Oct 2025). Proeduca delisted at EUR 1.54bn equity, c. 22x (2025). Below EUR 300m, BC Partners bought Metropolitan College (EUR 210m to 230m, c. 9x, 2023), Nextalia bought 24ORE Business School (EUR 200m, 2023), Jacobs bought Ilerna (>EUR 400m, 2024), Oakley bought Sigmund Freud University (2026), and GUS and Brightstar took Study Group out of Ardian in a distressed deal (May 2026). Refinancings dominate 2025-26: BPP GBP 710m TLB, Universidad Europea EUR 975m TLB with a EUR 300m dividend, Omnes c. EUR 700m, Galileo on S&P negative outlook.

## 5. Returns earned by prior sponsors

Disclosed or derivable returns are few. Those found:

- Palamon on 24ORE Business School: entry c. EUR 80m (2017, c. 17x), exit EUR 200m to Nextalia (2023); reported 3.9x and 29% IRR.
- Oakley on IU: EUR 85m equity for 79% (2017); 2023 sale to its own continuation vehicle generated GBP 240m of proceeds for Oakley Capital Investments and a reported 85% gross IRR; 2025-26 write-down.
- Providence on Galileo: Studialis bought for EUR 250m (2015); group sold at EUR 2.3bn to 2.5bn (2020), c. 18x EBITDA.
- Permira on Universidad Europea: EUR 770m entry (2018) to c. EUR 2.0bn to 2.2bn (2024) with 35% retained, roughly 2.6x to 2.9x on EV in five and a half years before leverage.
- CVC on UAX: c. EUR 1.1bn entry (2019) to c. EUR 2.0bn (2025), roughly 1.8x on EV in six years before leverage.
- CVC on Multiversity: EUR 225m for 50% at c. EUR 500m to 750m EV (2019) to EUR 3.97bn (2024), but to its own continuation fund.
- Laureate on Glion and Les Roches: CHF 380m (2016) after years of under-investment; Eurazeo has not exited after ten years.
- Capital destroyed: CapMan sold YrkesAkademin for SEK 1 after a reconstruction clearing SEK 585m of debt (2024); Ardian sold Study Group in a distressed deal with the legacy holding company put into administration (2026); INTO has negative equity after a thirteen-year minority hold by Leeds Equity.

Median disclosed EBITDA multiple across the four cleanly reported platform deals is c. 15.8x; large platforms cluster at 17x to 22x. Mid-market entries have been materially cheaper: Metropolitan College c. 9x, Multiversity's first tranche 10x to 15x, Groupe EDH 15x, Sommet 13.6x.

## 6. Key risks

- Regulatory licence risk is the dominant loss-of-capital risk. Examples from the last 24 months: Italy's DM 1835 teacher-ratio rules and the ANVUR finding against Pegaso; Spain's RD 905/2025 on new universities, annulled Oct 2026 and now being re-enacted; the UK franchise crackdown, levy and OfS pause on degree-awarding powers; Germany's §16b visa ruling against IU; Poland's 50% foreign-student cap; Greece's Council of State annulments; the French bill creating a two-tier approval with non-profit-only "intérêt général" status, adopted by the Senate June 2026.
- International student dependence. Cyprus, UK pathways, Polish privates and German online providers earn most growth from non-EU students. Policy turned in 2024 and has not turned back.
- Leverage at the platforms. Galileo (S&P B negative), IU (EUR 500m TLB due 2028, write-down), Omnes (c. 7x gross debt to EBITDA on refinancing), GUS (7.2x in 2020, TLB due 2027), Multiversity (B2/B) all carry structures that limit their ability to buy and may force unit sales.
- Public funding reversal. France's apprenticeship cuts and the UK fee cap and loan rules for franchised provision show how fast funded models deteriorate.
- Reputational events. Collegium Humanum in Poland (compulsory administration, convictions, full-tuition refunds), Link Campus and eCampus probes in Italy, UK loan-fraud investigations at franchised colleges.
- Entry price. The 2019 to 2024 large-cap multiples of 17x to 22x assumed growth that regulation is now capping; several sponsors are stuck (BPP, Sommet, AD Education, IU). The mid-market has cleared at 9x to 15x.

## 7. Recommended sourcing angle

The large platforms are either too big, recently traded, or over-levered. The actionable pool at EUR 50m to 300m has three shapes.

First, sponsor exits that are overdue or signalled. QA Higher Education (CVC, sale intent on record), BIMM (ICG, year six, refinanced), Cambridge Education Group (Bridgepoint, year twelve), Skill & You (IK, year five), Metropolitan College (BC Partners, in range, cheap entry), Digit'Ed (Nextalia), Swiss Education Group (Summer Capital, year eight). Approach the sponsor and the lenders directly; several of these will not run clean auctions.

Second, founder and family succession in Germany, Spain and Ireland. IRO Group's medical schools, ESO's ISM and Munich Business School, Cesur, UCJC, Nebrija, UEMC, UDIMA, Griffith College, Groupe Ynov, MediaSchool. These are the assets most likely to sell at 9x to 13x. The work is registry pulls (Bundesanzeiger, Registro Mercantil, CRO, Infogreffe) to size them, followed by principal-to-founder conversations.

Third, carve-outs from leveraged or non-core owners. Ensilis from EQT's Universidad Europea, Galileo's Nordic and German units, AD Education's SAE and ICMP, Sommet's École Ducasse, Planeta's French schools, Cognos's AMBIS, Salta's Tio or LOI, AcadeMedia's YH operators. These need a view on brand licences and shared services, and a seller under pressure, which Galileo, Omnes and IU now are.

Underwriting discipline should favour majority-domestic intake, licences that are scarce or hard to replicate (medicine, law, own degree-awarding powers, Italian telematic licences), limited reliance on public funding, and net leverage below 4x at entry. Norway, Spain, Germany, Greece and Cyprus rank highest on the country matrix for a for-profit control owner; the UK offers the deepest deal flow but the weakest regulatory tailwind in 2026-27.
